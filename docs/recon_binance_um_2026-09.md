## 6. Implications for the section-C.4 kill tests

**Funding-rate cross-section / carry:** 1096 days with an average of 108 symbols/day; cross-sectional dispersion (p90-p10) ranges ~4-39 bp/day across the window (highest in 2020-2021, cooling to single-digit bp by late 2021/2022) and rank persistence is 0.64/0.47/0.38 at lag 1/3/7 days - enough breadth and enough day-count for a cross-sectional funding-carry kill test, but funding is missing for 25 of the 229 pre-2023 symbols, and high funding shows a -0.09 rank correlation with trailing volume, i.e. any carry construction should stratify or control for cap/liquidity rather than treat the cross-section as homogeneous.

**Open-interest deleveraging:** 78 events >=20% across the 10 metrics symbols in this window (67 after excluding zero-OI feed-glitch artifacts -- see the data-integrity caveat in section 4), but metrics for the 9 alts only start 2021-12 (BTCUSDT alone covers 2020-09 to 2022-12) - event density and BTC-specific counts are usable now, a cross-sectional alt study effectively only has ~13 months (2021-12 to 2022-12) rather than the full window. Any kill-test implementation must exclude or repair the zero-OI hours before computing 24h OI-change features, or it will manufacture spurious collapse events.

**Listing/delisting effects:** 226 qualifying listing events (>=60d subsequent data) and 24 qualifying delisting events (>=60d prior data) before 2023-01-01 - this is the N available for a listing/delisting event study in the discovery window; delisting N in particular is thin (24) and any kill test built on it should treat results as indicative rather than well-powered.

**General caveat:** all of the above is descriptive - no forward returns, spreads, or Sharpe were computed in producing this report; a kill test still needs its own return-based design and its own holdout-respecting trial entry.
